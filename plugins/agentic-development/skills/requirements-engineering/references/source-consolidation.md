# Source consolidation

Use this method to recover requirements from conversations, issues, notes, and existing specifications. The result is a requirements record that a subsequent consumer can use without reconstructing the entire discussion.

## Establish the evidence

Locate the existing record and the source passages needed for the assignment. Read surrounding qualifications and later corrections. Record each source's location, speaker or role, and applicable authority where consequential. Treat a generated summary as a finding aid until its material claims have been checked against accessible originals. Missing originals limit what can be established.

Separate stakeholder needs and instructions from observations, examples, proposals, decisions, and explanations. Preserve uncertainty about the role of a statement when that role changes what a consumer must do. Publication or repetition does not turn an agent proposal into a stakeholder decision.

## Preserve the meaning

For each consequential statement, establish what it governs, under which conditions, and with what force: required, permitted, prohibited, preferred, or illustrative. Retain the relevant bound, exception, and decision authority. Use a short table or a precise sentence where that makes the relationships easier to inspect; ordinary factual notes need no elaborate metadata.

When multiple clauses interact, distinguish independent conditions from a sequence or prerequisite. Read the connective and its scope in the original passage. Adjacent instructions do not by themselves establish a dependency. An example may explain a rule without exhaustively specifying its cases.

Compare a proposed restatement with the original in both directions:

1. Find a relevant situation in which the source requires or permits something that the restatement loses.
2. Find a relevant situation in which the restatement adds an obligation or restriction that the source does not establish.
3. Check the surrounding source for an exception, scope qualification, or explicit dependency that resolves the difference.

Use these scenarios where an interpretation changes behavior, authority, or scope. A difference can be a defect in the restatement or a useful new proposal; classify it rather than silently treating it as source meaning. Preserve genuine ambiguity when the evidence supports multiple readings.

## Derive and reconcile

A faithful restatement and a derived requirement serve different purposes. For a derivation, connect the need to the proposed behavior through its rationale and assumptions. Identify where engineering judgment introduces a choice. Use [analysis and specification](analysis-specification.md) to develop it; source comparison does not require every derived requirement to be a literal paraphrase.

Reconcile duplicate statements without losing their qualifications. Distinguish a correction or superseding decision from an additional case. Apply the project's authority rules; recency alone does not grant a source authority to override another. Show unresolved conflicts with the competing meanings and the decisions they affect.

## Deliver a usable record

Use the existing artifact structure and [record discipline](record-discipline.md). Retain the current requirements, their significant sources and derivations, applicable decisions, and open matters. Keep discussion chronology in its source or change record. Preserve enough source context to check the meaning without copying private conversation detail into reusable guidance.

Identify the record's location, what work it supports, and which unresolved matters affect continuation. Source access and source comparison remain distinct from stakeholder validation or acceptance.
