# Elicitation

The work product is evidence about stakeholder needs and the consequential gaps that remain. An interview is one method, not a required form for every task.

## Select evidence by the gap

Recover known context and sources first. Use [context artifacts](context-artifacts.md) when discovering stakeholders, goals, domain concepts, or a system vision; use [usage and quality](usage-quality.md) when the gap concerns interactions or quality expectations. Identify whose work, interests, authority, or constraints are represented and which consequential perspective is missing. Treat candidate stakeholders as candidates until their relevance is established.

| Gap | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Unclear difficulty or desired outcome | A concrete episode, its consequence, and what a satisfactory outcome would change |
| Work differs from the written procedure | Observation, operational records, or a walkthrough with the people doing the work |
| Conflicting accounts or priorities | Each stakeholder's conditions and rationale, followed by an explicit tradeoff with the responsible decision maker |
| Unknown quality or constraint | Current conditions, tolerable outcomes, and the stakeholder or evidence that can establish a bound |
| A feature request is presented as the need | The benefit it serves, its failure conditions, and whether the mechanism is mandated |

Ask a focused question or present a distinguishing scenario that resolves the current gap. Adapt the next question to the answer rather than issuing an exhaustive questionnaire. Avoid supplying a preferred answer or treating silence as agreement.

Record findings with their sources and qualifications. Separate observed facts from explanations and preferences. Identify inaccessible evidence and its consequence. Hand substantive interpretation gaps to problem framing or analysis; stakeholder confirmation of the resulting requirements belongs to validation.
